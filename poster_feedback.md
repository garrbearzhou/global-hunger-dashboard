# Poster Feedback: Garrett Zhou - WFP Bangladesh

## Overall Assessment
The poster covers the right sections (Introduction, Challenge and Impact, Solutions and Recommendations, Acknowledgements) and includes strong model results. However, there are factual errors, a misleading statistic, missing visuals, and opportunities to make the content more impactful for a poster audience.

---

## Errors to Fix

### 1. "Maghna" → "Meghna"
Spelling error in the river name. It's the **Meghna** river.

### 2. Disaster Readiness: 1.000/1.000 (185/185) — MISLEADING
As presented, "1.000/1.000" looks like a **perfect score** (best possible). In reality, 1.000 on the ND-GAIN disaster preparedness sub-indicator means the **worst possible score** — zero preparedness. Bangladesh ranks dead last (185th out of 185). You need to clarify this. Suggested fix:
- "Disaster Preparedness: **Last place** (185/185) — zero preparedness score"
- Or: "Disaster Preparedness: 1.000 (worst possible; 185/185)"

### 3. "Bengalis" → "Bangladeshis"
"700,000 Bengalis were displaced annually" — the standard term in development/humanitarian writing is **Bangladeshis**, not Bengalis. "Bengali" refers to the ethnic/linguistic group (which includes West Bengal, India). "Bangladeshi" refers to citizens of Bangladesh.

### 4. ND-GAIN Index score needs context
"ND-Gain Index: 35.5/100 (174/185)" — the lower the score, the worse. A reader glancing at "35.5/100" might not know if that's good or bad. Add a brief qualifier:
- "ND-GAIN Index: 35.5/100 — **11th most vulnerable country** (174/185)"

### 5. "It's rich with culture" — minor
Contraction is fine for a poster, but "It is" or restructuring reads slightly more polished for an academic presentation.

## Content Improvements

### 6. Introduction is too thin
The current intro is three sentences with no data. Compare to the sample poster (Qatar), which immediately sets up the problem with specific context. Add one punchy stat to ground the reader:
- "Home to 173 million people in an area the size of North Carolina, Bangladesh is the 10th most densely populated country on Earth — and one of the most climate-vulnerable."

### 7. Missing key stats from your paper
The poster omits several powerful numbers that are in your paper:
- **Population**: 173 million (never stated on poster)
- **Undernourishment**: 11.9% / 18 million people
- **GDP**: $475B but 155th per capita
- **Sea level**: 17% of territory could be submerged by 2050
- **Adaptation buffer concept**: Your paper's most original contribution — not on the poster at all
- **$11.2B in capital investments** lifts 18M people; benefits persist for a generation

Consider adding a "By the Numbers" box with 4-5 key statistics.

### 8. No graphs or data visualizations
The poster has no charts. You have two publication-quality graphs ready:
- **vulnerability_graph.png** — shows the adaptation buffer narrative (Stagnation → Collapse → Recovery → Warning)
- **allocation_graph.png** — shows all 13 policies with investment and impact

These should be on the poster. The vulnerability graph in particular tells the entire story visually. Even one graph would dramatically strengthen the poster.

### 9. Image citation
"Bangladeshis fleeing flooding in Dhaka from canals. 1" — the "1" footnote needs a corresponding citation somewhere on the poster. Based on our earlier discussion, cite the image directly below it with the source (e.g., photographer/organization, publication, date). You don't need a separate Works Cited section on the poster, but the footnote "1" needs to resolve to something visible.

### 10. "known for corruption" is editorializing
"Awami League: 2009 - 2024 (known for corruption)" — this reads as opinion rather than fact on a poster. More neutral phrasing:
- "Awami League: 2009–2024 (CPI rank: 149/180)"
- Or: "Awami League: 2009–2024 — launched BDP 2100; CPI rank 149/180"

This conveys the same information (they were corrupt) but with data rather than a subjective label.

### 11. Awami League dates
"Awami League: 2009 - 2024" — technically, the Awami League (under various leaders) has been in power at other times too. What you mean is Sheikh Hasina's continuous tenure. Consider: "Awami League (Hasina): 2009–2024" to be precise.

## Structural Suggestions

### 12. Add a "Typical Family" element
The outline expects a typical family section. Your paper has an excellent second-person narrative. Even a 2-3 sentence summary box on the poster would satisfy this:
- "A typical Bangladeshi family lives in a rural area with 2-3 children. They farm rice for subsistence, earn ~$10/day, and cook over wood or crop waste. One-third lack access to clean drinking water."

### 13. Results section could be stronger
The model results are compressed into bullet points that lose their punch. Consider restructuring:
- **Headline finding**: "$11.2B in capital investment → food security for 18M people that persists for 20 years"
- **Key insight**: "Capital investments deliver 3.2x more food security per dollar than feeding programs over 20 years"
- **The vulnerability cliff**: "When feeding programs stop in year 6, impact drops 51% overnight — but capital investments keep producing"

### 14. Missing conclusion / call to action
The sample poster doesn't have one either, but your paper has a strong closing line: "A period of stagnation is not stability — it is accumulated fragility." This belongs on the poster as a closing statement.
