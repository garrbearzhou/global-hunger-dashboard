## 2. Country background & typical family (~15% — ~300 words)

**Country (use your app’s Data Coverage / Country Details for stats):**

- Population, % rural vs urban.
- How many people are food insecure / undernourished (cite FAO/undernourishment from your app if you use it).
- Government type; major crops (rice, wheat, vegetables, fish); share of land cultivated.
- Climate and geography: delta, monsoon, floods, cyclones, salinity in coastal areas, drought in some regions.

**Typical family:**

- Average household size; who works (farming, day labor, small business).
- Diet: rice, lentils, fish, vegetables; what’s available in village vs city.
- Where they get food (own farm, market, relief) and how they cook (fuel, water).
- Access to education, health care, clean water, sanitation, roads, markets.
- One concrete detail (e.g. a coastal family losing crops to salinity or a flood).

**Sources:** World Bank, FAO, WFP/GRFC reports, Bangladesh government or UN country briefs. You can cite your app’s underlying sources (FAO, etc.).

## 3. Challenge and impact on food security (~25% — ~500 words)

**Climate change in Bangladesh:**

- **Present status:** More intense floods, cyclones, salinity intrusion, erratic rainfall, heat stress. Use your app’s climate vulnerability index / disaster data if you reference it.
- **Trends:** Are shocks increasing in frequency or severity? (You can use your app’s disaster or time-series data if relevant.)
- **Drivers:** Sea-level rise, warmer seas (cyclones), glacier melt and river flow, land use.

**Impact on food security (use the four pillars if helpful):**

- **Availability:** Crops damaged by floods, salinity, or drought; fisheries affected; supply chains disrupted.
- **Access:** Prices spike after disasters; jobs and incomes lost; poor and landless hit hardest.
- **Utilization:** Contaminated water after floods; health and nutrition suffer.
- **Stability:** Repeated shocks prevent recovery; people sell assets, fall into debt.

**Who is affected most:**

- Rural vs urban (e.g. farmers and fishing communities vs city dwellers).
- Coastal vs inland (salinity, cyclones vs river floods).
- Marginalized groups (e.g. landless, women, ethnic minorities).

**Sources:** IPCC or Bangladesh climate reports, FAO GIEWS, WFP/GRFC, your app’s indicators (undernourishment, climate vulnerability, food supply). At least 1–2 in-text citations here.

## 4. Exploring solutions (~25% — ~500 words)

Describe **2–3** approaches that address climate-related food insecurity:

1. **Climate-smart agriculture (CSA)**  
   Stress-tolerant crops (e.g. saline-tolerant rice, flood-tolerant varieties), floating gardens, better water management, diversification. What’s been tried in Bangladesh? (e.g. BRAC, government programs.) Strengths and limits.

2. **Early warning and disaster preparedness**  
   Cyclone shelters, flood forecasting, evacuation, emergency food and cash. Who runs these? Impact on lives and food security.

3. **Adaptive infrastructure and social protection**  
   Embankments, drainage, irrigation; crop insurance or cash transfers after shocks. One example from Bangladesh or the region.

- Do these meet the needs of different groups (e.g. farmers, landless, women)?
- What strengths or resources does Bangladesh have (e.g. NGOs, research, community groups)?

**Sources:** Government plans, BRAC, IFPRI, FAO, WFP, academic or NGO reports. Cite at least 1–2.

---

## 5. Your recommendation (~25% — ~500 words)

**Choose one solution** (or a clear combination, e.g. “scaling saline-tolerant rice + early warning in coastal areas”).

- **What:** Concrete plan (what will be done, where, for whom).
- **Impact you hope for:** e.g. fewer crop losses, more stable incomes, better food access after shocks.
- **Limitations:** Cost, capacity, time, equity (who might be left out).
- **Who leads:** Government, NGOs, research institutes, communities.
- **Resources/funding:** Where could money come from? (e.g. climate funds, donors, government.)
- **Policies needed:** e.g. support for stress-tolerant seeds, early-warning systems, insurance.
- **Cultural/context:** Why this fits Bangladesh (e.g. reliance on rice, history of cyclones, strong NGO sector).
- **Sustainability:** Will it last beyond one project? Can it be scaled?

Keep it feasible and specific (e.g. “Scale up saline-tolerant rice and community early warning in 2–3 coastal districts” rather than “fix climate change”).
